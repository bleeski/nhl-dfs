# C39 first commit: how stale is the history store, and what would current games change (2026-10-06)

Measurement only. The code in this commit reads the store and prints a line; it changes no lineup, and `tests/test_history_currency.py`
shows the exported file is byte-identical with and without it. Generated by `scripts/c39_measure.py` from a scratch copy of the store
(`cp -rp`, so write times survive) under an audit hook that refuses any write inside the repo's `data/`, `runs/` and `outputs/` and
blocks the network (except the one scratch-only look in section 5). The real store, the real raw cache, `data/standings`,
`data/ledger` and `data/entered` were not written.

## What the numbers say, in plain English

1. **The store stops in June.** Newest game of any type 2026-06-14 (a playoff game), newest regular-season game 2026-04-16, files last
   written 2026-09-28 about 8:53 pm Chicago time. At the as-of date 2026-10-06 that is 173 days behind on regular-season games.
   Players whose newest regular game is within 14 days of the as-of date: **0 of 1,069**.
2. **The 2026-27 regular season opened 2026-09-29, and the store has none of it.** The cached NHL schedule lists 43 regular-season
   games from 09-29 to 10-05 (5, 3, 8, 5, 13, 5, 4 per day). A real look at the NHL reports, into scratch folders only, fetched
   exactly that: **43 games, 1,548 skater rows and 88 goalie rows, in 8 requests and 6.6 seconds**. With those rows the scratch store
   reads CURRENT (newest regular game 2026-10-05). So catching up costs about 7 seconds, far inside the 20 s budget planned for it.
3. **Tonight's loss is small.** For the 2026-10-06 slate (18 teams): back-to-back goalie flags go from 0 to 1 (OTT, the only team that
   also played 10-05); a goalie's start share moves by 0.028 on average; skaters' even-strength shot rate moves by a median 1.3%, and
   1% of skaters move by more than 10%. The store is a week behind a season that is a week old, so there is not much yet to miss.
4. **It grows with the season.** The replay below, on real 2025-26 history, compares a store frozen at the previous season's close
   with the real store as of each date. One and two weeks in the gap is small (1% to 2% of skaters move by more than 10%, goalie
   start shares move 0.04 to 0.06). By 2026-01-15 it is 22% of skaters and 0.13 of start share; by 2026-03-01 it is 29% and 0.24, and
   the back-to-back flag fires for 4 to 6 goalies on a slate day instead of none.
5. **A cloud session has no store at all** (data/features and data/raw are not in git), so none of the above is visible there; that is
   what the third commit addresses.

What this does not tell you: these are changes in the model's **features** (shot rates, start shares, flags), not in lineup points
or winnings. The card's "lineup gain unmeasured" stands. The numbers support doing the chunk (the feature gap is real and grows weekly)
and say plainly that today's slate gains little; the value is mostly for the weeks ahead and for cloud runs.

### A correction made to the replay, not to the chunk

The first run of this table listed, for each team, only the goalies who played on the slate day. That reported **0 -> 0** back-to-back
flags on all four dates and looked like the flag was dead. It was the replay: a goalie who started last night and then rests tonight (the
usual back-to-back outcome) was never in the list, so the flag had no goalie to attach to; the real pool lists both goalies. Checked
directly on the 2025-26 store: 430 team-games came on consecutive nights, 35 of them with the same goalie in both games, and the flag
fired in 10 of 10 spot checks on those. The replay now lists every goalie who played for the team in the prior 14 days. The first
run's other columns were almost the same (p_start shift 0.002, 0.000, 0.024, 0.000). The design of the chunk did not change.
