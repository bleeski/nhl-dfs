# C17 replay of the 2026-09-30 Classic slate (backlog B51, B63)

Recorded 2026-10-06 by `scripts/c17_replay.py`. Question: when the ownership prior is given the slate's odds, role state, news ages and
goalie confirmations, does the forecast move toward what the field did, and does settle's ownership error (`mae_all`) rise against the
09-30 record? Nothing here is fitted: no weight, floor or gate was changed to produce a number.

## What was replayed

- Inputs: the 09-30 run's own `inputs/DKSalaries.csv` and `DKEntries.csv` (run 20260930-210607-classic, first game 23:30Z) and the
  09-30 standings (four contests: 196218433, 196218434, 196218438, 196228907), copied to a scratch folder.
- Clock: 2026-09-30 21:06:30Z. Every web page the engine asked for was served from `data/raw` as it stood at that moment, found through
  `data/raw/observations/*.jsonl` (newest body fetched at or before the clock): partner odds 60fe509f (as of 21:00Z, 3 games),
  Daily Faceoff team pages COL eac189af, LAK dad41ed6, NYI 18e71a22, PHI 38d9cc18, PIT 594f46c9 (19:30Z) and TOR a13ede25 (21:06Z),
  the goalie page 03b25d9c (21:06Z). DraftKings pages answered 403 again, as in the record.
- Guards: an audit hook blocked the network and refused any write outside the scratch folder. Result in every run: 0 unpinned URLs
  requested, 0 network attempts, 0 refused writes. Ben's `data/` and `runs/` were only read.
- One seed for every arm: the run's own default (from the salary file hash). Nothing was rerun with another seed.
- World A ("as the original run saw it", the gate): no lobby capture or cached contest tables, so every contest keeps the family prior and
  a 5,000-lineup field, as in the record. World B ("as today's code would see it"): the lobby capture and cached tables as of the clock were
  copied in, so C16 and C38 act. B differs from A by at most 0.001 in `mae_all`, so only A is tabled.
- Grading is the grade function settle uses (`read_salary`, `standings.read_all`, `standings.join`, `forecast_from_run`, `grade`), called
  read-only on each replay's `scenario/fields.json`. `settle` is never pointed at Ben's folders (it writes to the ledger, BACKLOG.md and the standings folder). As a cross-check the real
  `settle.run` was run once on the world A all-inputs replay with its runs root, ledger, BACKLOG.md, standings copy and cache all in the
  scratch folder (same guards: 0 refused writes, 0 network attempts); its ownership grades are identical, 3.773, 3.652, 4.183, 3.652.
- Arms switch parts of the field inputs off by wrapping `field_inputs.collect` (the script, not the product, has these switches); "off" is
  the product's own `field_inputs.enabled: false`.

## How to read it

1. **The exit check as typed passes, but only with a fix made after seeing replay numbers.** The all-inputs arm is below the recorded
   `mae_all` in all four contests (3.773 against 4.106, 3.652 against 4.034, 4.183 against 4.315, 3.652 against 3.942). The same arm
   without the imputation of point 6 (the first design, last row of the tables) is above the record in contest 196218438 (4.543 against
   4.315) and below it in the other three (4.034, 3.913, 3.856). So the literal gate is passed because of a design change adopted after
   looking at this slate's numbers; read it as a pass with a post-hoc fix, not as independent evidence.
2. **That comparison flatters C17.** The recorded run priced 112 of its 133 persons on PRIOR (manifest `model.counts`), because the DK codes
   of COL, LAK, NYI, PHI and PIT were not yet verified for the history crosswalk (B35, fixed 2026-10-02). Today's code on the same
   inputs has 22 PRIOR persons of 127 and scores 3.73, 3.61, 3.91 and 3.63 with the inputs switched off. The honest measure of C17 is all
   inputs against off, on the same code.
3. **C17's own effect on this slate is a small worsening.** All against off: `mae_all` +0.045, +0.045, +0.271, +0.023; Pearson
   0.808 to 0.769, 0.811 to 0.766, 0.792 to 0.710, 0.804 to 0.782. One slate, three of six Daily Faceoff pages usable, placeholder weights;
   not a basis to tune (C12 fits weights behind its gate) and not evidence the inputs are wrong. B63's own acceptance (ownership error on
   the next five settled slates does not rise) is the real test and stays as a deferred trigger.
4. **By input (full size).** Goalie confirmations improved all four contests (-0.08, -0.11, -0.16, -0.02). Odds alone worsened them
   (+0.36, +0.31, +0.07, +0.45) while moving the stack forecast toward the actual lineups for Colorado (31 to 54, actual 38 to 51),
   Philadelphia (13 to 25, actual 25 to 27) and Toronto (20 to 13, actual 14 to 24). PP1, line 1 and news are small and mixed. The
   inputs partly offset each other: all inputs together (+0.10 on average) is better than odds alone (+0.30) or roles alone (+0.23).
5. **Coverage.** At the clock only 3 of 6 slate pages were inside the 24 h age policy (`models/roles.py`), so 15 players had a PP1 flag and
   15 a line-1 flag and 3 teams read as the covered average (see the imputation note). C40 (keep the last known lines) is the queued fix.
6. **Imputation note (disclosure).** The first version scored a skater on a team with no usable page as "not on PP1 or line 1". A small
   diagnostic replay (400-draw field, not the gate size) showed the roles arm raising `mae_all` by 0.3 to 0.9 with 3 of 6 teams covered.
   The cause is a coverage artifact (only covered teams could get the boosts), so unknown now reads as the covered average of his position
   group. That is one binary design choice with no tunable number, motivated by the bias argument and covered by a unit test, and no weight
   was touched. But it was adopted after seeing replay numbers on the same slate that is the gate. Measured afterwards at full size, the
   first design (all inputs, no imputation) worsens `mae_all` against off by +0.31, +0.31, +0.63, +0.23 (average +0.37) and the
   imputation brings that to +0.045, +0.045, +0.271, +0.023 (average +0.10): most of the first design's worsening was the artifact.
   At the small size: all-inputs went from 4.179, 4.063, 4.752, 4.026 to 3.999, 3.857, 4.354, 3.864 (off: 3.839, 3.741, 4.075, 3.773).

## B51: the Colorado trace and the portfolio

- Cause: the 09-30 DK pool lists only 11 Colorado forwards (expected to dress 9.6, or 9.3 with the role state), under the hard floor of 10
  forwards in `sim/market.model_strength`, so Colorado took the league goal rate 3.046 although its listed skaters model at 3.39, the
  highest of the six teams. The fallback lowered the strongest team.
- Fix: the team's own mean skater goals per game over its last 82 regular-season games before the slate (`ParamTable.team_goals`):
  COL 3.634, PIT 3.537, TOR 3.073, PHI 2.927, NYI 2.793, LAK 2.683. LAK@COL now prints `COL: listed skaters cover 9.6 F and 6.0 D, its
  own goal rate 3.63 over its last 82 games used`.
- Replay: LAK@COL, NYI@TOR and PIT@PHI all price on MARKET (B35's codes). The all-inputs portfolio holds Colorado skaters in 3 of 5
  entries (1, 0, 2, 0, 1 per entry) and no stack of 3 or more; the inputs-off portfolio holds 4, 3, 5 and 1 Colorado skaters in four
  entries. The record's portfolio held none. B51's acceptance is met by its "or" clause (the fallback is named and replaced) and by
  Colorado being present; a 3-skater Colorado stack in the all-inputs portfolio is not reached.

  priced, total 6.5): the market's away intensity is 3.64. The 10-01 run's model figure was 2.67 (B66, x1.36). With the model side counted
  as history-backed the clip is the old 35%: away 3.64 is clipped to 3.60, the total is 6.846 against the market's 6.881 and P(home win)
  0.348 against 0.343, which is the 10-01 report's "CAPPED" case. With the model side on a salary prior the fit reproduces the market: total
  6.881, P(home win) 0.343, no CAPPED note. At a history share of 0.5 the limit is 68% and the game is not clipped. Which of these the
  real 10-01 game was, is not known on this machine (that run was built in a cloud session and its manifest is not here); the
  model-side figures above were taken from B66's text, so they are a reproduction, not a measurement.
